When an iron nail is dipped into a copper
sulphate solution, which substance gets
displaced? B
1 Copper ©
2 Copper Sulphate
3 = Sulphate
4 Iron
Solution

+ When an iron nail is placed in
a copper sulphate solution, a
displacement reaction occurs. This
is because iron is more reactive than
copper in the reactivity series of
metals.

+ Iron atoms lose electrons and go
into the solution as Fe~* ions, while
the copper ions (Cu2*) from copper
sulphate gain these electrons and
get deposited as metallic copper on
the nail.

+ The blue colour of the copper
sulphate solution gradually fades
because Cu** ions are removed, and
a reddish-brown layer of copper
forms on the iron nail.

+ Reaction Equation — Fe(s) +
CuSO, (aq) > FeSO 4(aq) + Cu(s).
Here, copper is displaced from the
copper sulphate solution.

+ Copper sulphate as a whole
isn’t displaced (only copper is).
Sulphate (SO ,”)) remains in the
solution bonded with Fe. Iron is
not displaced; instead, it replaces
copper.

Hence, the correct answer is "Copper".

Which of the following substances is
commonly used in the saponification process
to produce soap? B

1 Sodium bicarbonate ©

2 Sodium chloride

3 Sodium hydroxide

4 Sodium carbonate
Solution

+ Saponification is the chemical
process of making soap by reacting
fats or oils (triglycerides) with a
strong base (alkali).

* Sodium hydroxide (NaOH),
commonly known as lye or caustic
soda, is the primary substance used
in this process to produce solid or
"hard" bars of soap.

+ During the reaction, sodium
hydroxide breaks the ester bonds
in the fat molecules, resulting in
the formation of glycerol and the
sodium salts of fatty acids, which is
the soap itself.
